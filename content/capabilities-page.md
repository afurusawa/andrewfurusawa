## What I do

I take software products from concept to production in weeks instead of quarters, and I do it running the engineering process a full team would use. Agentic coding and LLM-assisted workflows are what make the pace possible, but what keeps the work solid is the discipline around it. Scoped requirements, written acceptance criteria, code review, and test coverage. Speed and quality are the same problem, not a tradeoff. What I hand off is something your engineers can own.

## Where I help

- **AI-accelerated product ownership.** I sit between stakeholders and engineering so the wrong thing doesn't get built, then build it. That means turning what people say they want into what the software has to do, surfacing the assumptions nobody stated, and validating it against real users through usability testing and discovery before it becomes expensive to change. Information architecture, wireframes, and prototypes come out of that work, along with the PRDs, user stories, and acceptance criteria the build runs on. Then I build against them with agentic tooling, which is where the speed comes from. The process underneath stays the same as any team would run, with code standards, code review, branch strategy, CI/CD, decision records, and a test strategy that decides what actually warrants coverage instead of chasing a percentage. Most wasted engineering effort traces back to a requirement everyone thought they understood the same way.
- **Multi-platform software with LLM integration.** Web, iOS, and Android from one codebase in React and Next.js, Ionic and Angular, or Flutter, chosen to fit what your team already runs, including moving existing apps onto a modern stack without stopping delivery. Where AI belongs in the product itself, I handle feasibility research, prompt architecture, and production implementation on a provider-agnostic foundation, so you are not locked into whichever model is winning this quarter.

## Recent work

- **Education, 2026.** Took an AI training product from an open question to a working application in under a month, solo. Researched whether LLMs could support competitive debate practice at all, then designed the prompt architecture that holds models to each phase of a round and converted judge scoring paradigms into a rubric returning both points and usable critique. Shipped provider-agnostic across two model vendors, with opposing models able to debate each other, phase timers, and speech input and output paced to competition time limits.
- **Healthcare, 2020–2024.** Hired to maintain a legacy iOS app running in three hospitals. Proposed and led the multi-platform rework that replaced it, carried it into production across more than ten hospitals nationwide, and eliminated a recurring hardware vendor licensing cost by implementing the capability natively. Introduced the development process, documentation, and specifications the team had been operating without.
- **Consumer IoT, 2017–2020.** Led a distributed front-end and QA team on connected-device apps serving 20,000+ users, improved load time by over 70% following an acquisition, and consolidated a fragmented device portfolio into one app platform.

## How I work

- **Discovery first.** Engagements start with a short paid discovery sprint: I dig into the problem, prove or disprove the risky assumption, and come back with a plan and a realistic scope. Small commitment for you, and the larger engagement gets built on something real.
- **Sprint-based, not spec-based.** Software product work rarely survives contact with a fixed specification, because you learn what is possible by building it. Short cycles, each with a working deliverable and written acceptance criteria, mean you see progress as it happens instead of finding out at the end whether it worked. Neither of us wants a surprise on delivery day.
- **Handoff is the deliverable.** Every engagement ends with documentation, specifications, and a codebase your team can maintain.
